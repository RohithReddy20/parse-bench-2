# Forms: repairs from the typed-in overlay (word alignment, comb values, check boxes)

Date: 2026-11-25. No GPU, no model calls: OCRia-1's published readings (stand-ins) corrected with what the PDF
says. Groundwork: `2026-11-25-forms-findings.md`. Numbers are the forms dimension (87.5% `forms`, 12.5%
`fillable_forms`) from the harness scorer, through `evalkit run/score/paired` on the two subsets; "dev" is the
development part, "check" the check slice (121 of the 500 `forms` pages). P is the paired bootstrap probability
of a gain over documents on the development part, against the configuration in the "base" column. Every run
includes the looped-read fallback (`fallback_below: 0.5`), which is the starting point (82.39).

## 1. What was built

- **`fixes/align.py`: which markdown word is which PDF word.** The read's visible tokens (tags, entities, box
  marks and image references removed) are folded to lowercase letters and digits and aligned with the PDF text
  layer: whole tokens equal to whole PDF words in order first (difflib on token lists), then characters inside
  the gaps between those anchors (so `Post code` matches `Postcode` and `2 2 8 6 2` matches one word `22862`),
  then long runs that occur once on the page out of order. A one- or two-letter piece of a match counts only
  as a whole token or a whole word. Before aligning, the PDF's phrases (words consecutive in content order on
  one line) are sorted into the read's order where a phrase is spelled exactly once in the markdown: some
  forms (US government forms) store their text in no reading order at all, and an in-order alignment then
  matched `U.S. citizen` to the wrong copy. Alignment takes 0.05 to 0.4 s on the largest pages.
- **`fixes/forms.py`: fills and their place in the read.** Overlay words are grouped into fills (one typed
  value each): words on one line, close together (up to 0.8 heights, or 1.6 glyph widths for typewriter fonts),
  with no printed word between them (dot leaders and lone signs do not count); single glyphs up to 2.6 heights
  apart form a comb, cut where the gap jumps (day, month and year boxes stay three values). Each fill gets an
  anchor: the printed word a reader passes just before it (nearest word to its left, else the end of the label
  above). The fill's words are inserted right after the anchor in the PDF word order, so the alignment places
  each typed value next to its label even when the same value is typed several times. 92% of the 7,118 fills
  on the 450 filled pages are placed on markdown tokens.
- **`pdf/boxes.py`: drawn check boxes.** Small nearly square vector paths (4 to 20 points), nested borders
  merged. 82% of the overlay's tick glyphs sit inside one.
- **Option `combs`.** A comb value the read spells glyph by glyph (`£ 2 2 8 6 2 · 0 0`) is written as one
  string (`£ 22862 · 0 0`), where only spaces, emphasis closed and reopened (`** **`, `</u> <u>`), a printed
  separator (`1 8 , 4 3 3` becomes `18,433`) or a character box's outline read as `[ ]` sit between the glyphs.
  Glyphs read one per table cell become one cell spanning those columns. A pipe between glyphs is a column
  and is left alone.
- **Option `boxes`** (`fixes/forms_boxes.py`). Every box mark of the read (`[x]`, `[ ]`, `☐`, `○`, `✓`, ...)
  gets its option label: the tokens beside it on its line or cell, after the box when the line ends with a
  label, before it when the line ends with a box. The label's first word must be aligned to a printed word. Each drawn box, and each tick outside any box, belongs
  to the closest label on its line that has it on the side the read writes boxes. A mark whose label owns a
  box with a tick, or a tick, is written checked; one whose label owns only empty boxes is written unchecked;
  any other mark keeps the read's state. A tick the read wrote as text right beside a decided mark
  (`Yes [ ] **X** No [ ]`) is removed.

Both options act only on a page whose last content stream is an overlay (450 of 450 filled `forms` pages, no
blank page, no `fillable_forms` page, 3 pages elsewhere in the benchmark); every other page writes back
byte-identical. They are on in `configs/standin_forms.yaml`.

## 6. Notes against the brief and the findings

- The findings' largest repair (printed labels left out, `COUNTRY CODE: 61`) did not hold up: the reference
  captions are often not printed text, and where the read left a label out it usually wrote its own caption in
  other words, so an inserted label made a second caption and moved the value out of the first one's slot
  (-0.48, and -0.02 with every guard tried).
- `RenderedPage.text_words` joins characters across a backward jump in x on one line when PDFium reports no
  space between them, so an overlay value drawn right after printed text on its line can be merged into a
  printed word (seen only in a synthetic test; 4,510 of 4,513 reference values are separate overlay words). Left
  as is: other fixes read `text_words`.
- Target 87.5 is not reached: 83.00 on all pages (82.73 dev, 83.84 check).

## 7. Next

The remaining loss is mostly where the read puts a value relative to its caption, so the next step is a
structural one: on pages with an overlay, rewrite a form table row as `caption: value` when the alignment shows
the row's typed value and its printed caption on one PDF line (the `| Office expenses | 8810 | 1,649.27 |`
case, and values read in a row below their caption). The alignment and the fill placement built here give
every piece that rewrite needs; it was not attempted because it replaces the read's structure rather than
correcting it, and needs its own guard against breaking tables the scorer reads correctly today.

## R2.1 What the scorer needed (found by scoring single pages)

- **496 of the 500 `forms` pages are scored from `form_field` rules, not from a form tree.** There are no reference
  tables on those pages: every field is a flat label such as `8 Advertising`, `RN2 Deduzione per abitazione
  principale` or `4.6 Logement — Surface existante avant travaux (A)`, located as a phrase. Grid mapping of
  output tables never helps them.
- **A caption that is not exact is only found in reading order.** The scorer picks one occurrence per caption by a
  chain in reference order and gives captions the chain leaves out only an exact occurrence. The rules list fields
  in an order of their own (column by column on one page, row by row on the next), so a caption the read writes
  without its printed line number (`| 8 | Advertising |`: the number in a cell of its own) is either garbled or,
  out of order, not found at all; `8 Advertising` in one cell is exact and is found anywhere.
- **A printed number in the caption's slot is read as the value.** `| 8 | Advertising | 8 | 1,597 |` (the number
  printed again at the box) and `| Salaries | 9060 | 2,149.10 |` (a box code) read `8` and `9060`: a critical
  wrong value. The scorer skips such a code column only when the codes match a printed id of a caption it found
  with the id outside the caption, which joining the id into the caption takes away. Hence both moves together.
- **Shared captions are the main reason blank fields go missing.** 57% of the remaining "empty field omitted"
  events and 49% of the "value without its caption" events have an exact occurrence of the label or of its last
  segment (`Plus-value`, `Surface totale`) in the read, which the chain gives to another field with the same
  caption: grids with a caption per column and a field per cell. Which order the rules list those cells in (row by row, column by column, filled ones first) is
  not predictable from the page.
- Only 185 of 712 failing filled fields have their reference caption printed right beside the typed value on the
  PDF (match ≥ 0.6 against the printed words left of or above the value). A "field lines from the PDF geometry"
  rewrite would reach at most those.

  caption cell, is joined to that caption (`| | 8 Advertising | | 1,597 |`); the same number printed again before

  than one value slot and one is filled (columns of amounts).
- **`codes`**: a printed box code (`9060`, `3.111`, `RN33`) right of a caption, followed by a typed value or by

- **`outlines`**: box marks right before a typed value with only a currency sign before them on its line or cell

- **`trailing`**: printed text the read wrote after a typed value on the same line, standing 4 glyph heights or more

## R3.1 Where the critical events come from (found by instrumenting the scorer)

I recorded which reading the scorer chose for every value event of `f3-base` (by wrapping `forms_v2.Reading` and
`locate`), where that reading sits relative to the caption, and whether its words are printed or typed on the PDF:

| Event (on `f3-base`) | Events | Rest of the caption's line | Rest of the caption's table cell | Next cell right | Cell below | Words printed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| invented value (critical) | 249 | 122 | 96 | 22 | 9 | 219 |
| wrong value (critical) | 199 | 73 | 69 | 48 | 9 | 140 |
| printed text in a blank field (minor) | 460 | 141 | 80 | 216 | 16 | 432 |

So most critical events are the **rest of the caption's own line or cell**, and the gap between the caption and that
reading is usually a plain space (no colon, no blank marker), so a rule cannot know where the caption ends. What a
rule can use is how the scorer forgives printed text:

- a run of at most 12 tokens with no digits between a caption and a colon that is followed by a blank marker is a
  "label tail" and is skipped (`Name of branch: [___]` reads blank, wherever the caption ends inside it);
- such a run that is one bracket group is skipped even with digits (`(127): [___]`, `(999 999-9999): [___]`);
- a worded bracket right after the caption is skipped (`(Beispiel Freifrau, Graf)`, but not with a colon inside);
- a printed code before `: [___]` labels the blank (`F22: [___]`);
- a caption that ends its line reads blank or the next line, and next-line readings may always read blank;
- a caption that opens a table cell reads the next cell, weakly (blank allowed) when its own cell holds a slot mark.

The repairs below write blank fields in those forms. They move text only where every moved word is aligned to a
printed (non-overlay) word of the PDF and nothing on the line or in the cell is typed.

## R3.2 What was built (`fixes/forms_slots.py`)

- **`blanks`** (lines with blank markers `[___]`, nothing typed on the line):
  - printed text after a marker starts a line of its own (one field per line: `First Name: [___] Middle Name: [___]`
    becomes two lines), except a continuation of a sentence (`and [___], which`, `the [___] — a specialist`, `[___]?`)
    or a date part (`Day: [___] Month: [___]`); a unit after a marker (`[___] km`) stays;
  - printed text between a caption's last colon and its marker becomes a bracketed hint before the colon
    (`Behinderung: 1 = Ja [___]` → `Behinderung (1 = Ja): [___]`); a lone box code there moves to the caption's front
    (`Arbeitslohn: 127 [___]` → `127 Arbeitslohn: [___]`);
  - between a caption and its colon, a box code at the end moves to the front and an italic hint or a bracketed format
    hint is bracketed (`gain imposable *Après abattement* **1TZ**:` → `1TZ gain imposable (Après abattement):`,
    `Telephone Number (999) 999-9999:` → `Telephone Number (999 999-9999):`); a line that opens with its own id keeps
    a code at the end (it is read as the id), and a caption's own trailing number stays (`Attach Form 4797`);
  - a colon inside a bracketed hint is dropped (`Namenszusatz (Beispiel: Freifrau, Graf):`);
  - a caption in two parts (an emphasised run then plain words, or two scripts) gets a colon before its marker
    (`支店・事業所名 Name of branch [___]` → `…Name of branch: [___]`); a one-part caption does not, since a colon
    makes the scorer prefer that occurrence of a repeated caption (it stole `Applicant(s) relationship to the child`
    from the filled block on `C100 p8`); a choice word (`Yes`, `No`, `oui`) is never moved.
- **`cells`** (table cells holding only printed text, in rows without check boxes): the same for a cell with a
  marker; a cell ending in a box's decoration gets it as a bracketed hint plus a blank marker (`Totale credito 5 ,00`
  → `Totale credito (5 ,00): [___]`, `RETENIDO ( 4 )` → `RETENIDO (4): [___]`, `(col. 7 minus col. 21) 9` →
  `(col. 7 minus col. 21 9): [___]`), and a cell ending in a worded hint gets the marker after it
  (`Branch number (5 digits): [___]`, `Geburtsort (… falls Ausland auch Staat angeben): [___]`). Not inside a word
  (`IHT401`, `W-2`, `assuré(e)`, `May. 2020`), and not where the right neighbour is empty or missing and the cell
  below holds printed text (an emptied slot then reads that cell: `Balance owing 48500` on `T1-5006-R p8`).
- **`blank_forms`**: `blanks` on pages without an overlay when the read writes at least 3 blank markers (blank forms
  and AcroForm pages); every word aligned to the PDF counts as printed there. Table cells are left alone on such
  pages: without typed values a data table looks like a grid of printed captions (`May. 2020` in a rate table).
- `hints` (built, not kept): the decoration rule and a short printed tail after a colon on lines without a marker.

The slot step runs last in `forms.repair`, on a fresh alignment of the repaired markdown (0.05 to 0.4 s per filled
page).

## R3.6 Next

- **Header rows over one row of typed values**: when a table's first row is printed captions and the only other row
  holds the typed values under them, write one `caption: value` line per column. Unlike round two's grid rewrite
  (many rows, where the rules' field order is a lottery), one data row is unambiguous, and it targets the 39 wrong
  values read from the next header cell.
- **Long instructions in a caption's cell**: the scorer skips at most 12 words of label tail; a rule needs the
  caption's end, and the PDF's fonts may give it (captions bold or larger than the instruction after them on many of
  these forms; `RenderedPage.text_words` can carry weights with `styles=True`).
- **Blank forms' table cells**: `cells` on pages without an overlay gained +0.02 but rewrites data tables; a gate on
  drawn empty boxes beside the cell (the PDF's vector graphics) would tell a form's caption cell from a data cell.
